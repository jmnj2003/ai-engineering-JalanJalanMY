## Section 0 — Project Framing & Application Scope Checkpoint

| Framing element | JalanJalanMY |
|---|---|
| **Problem Evidence** | **1. AI travel tools give wrong or non-existent recommendations.** In an August 2026 survey of U.S. leisure travellers, 16% of those who used AI for travel were recommended a place, tour or activity that did not exist, and 55% received at least one bad recommendation. Incorrect prices, hours or details was the most common problem (27%). Source: Greetwell survey via Stacker/KEYT, 16 Sept 2026 — https://keyt.com/lifestyle/travel/stacker-travel/2026/09/16/survey-ai-hallucinated-travel-destinations-for-1-in-6-users/ (note: run by a travel concierge company). <br>**2. Malaysia's visitor market is large and growing.** 42.2 million visitor arrivals in 2025 (+11.2% from 38 million in 2024); Visit Malaysia 2026 targets about 45 million visitors. Sources: https://says.com/my/news/malaysia-records-42-2-million-visitors-in-2025 ; https://www.ipohecho.com.my/2025/04/30/malaysia-sets-sights-on-45-million-tourists-rm270-billion-in-revenue-for-vm2026/ <br>Together: many people plan Malaysian trips, and the AI tools they increasingly use are unreliable on exactly the facts a plan depends on (hours, prices, existence). |
| **Existing Approaches** | **1. TravelPlanner benchmark** (Xie et al., ICML 2024) — evaluates LLM agents on multi-constraint travel planning; shows GPT-4 reached only a 0.6% success rate. <br>**2. LLM + formal verification** (Hao et al., arXiv:2404.11891) — the LLM converts the request into a constraint problem for a solver; 93.9% success on TravelPlanner, but on synthetic U.S. data. <br>**3. Official tourism portals** (Tourism Malaysia e-brochures, state tourism boards) — authoritative attraction information, but static, split by state and not personalised. <br>**4. Itinerary apps** (e.g. Wanderlog) — help users arrange chosen places, but rely on the user or generic web data and do not verify plans against official opening hours. |
| **Project / Engineering Gap** | LLMs can personalise trips but cannot reliably satisfy hard constraints; official Malaysian tourism information is reliable but fragmented across national and state sources, not personalised, and covers only part of what tourists want to visit (for example, popular cafés and food spots); existing planners do not check whether a day — especially a cross-state day — is physically possible. The practical gap is **poor grounding and missing feasibility checking** for personalised Malaysian itineraries. (This is an engineering gap, not a claim of scientific novelty.) |
| **Objective O1** | To design a grounded itinerary planner for Malaysia that recommends only verifiable places — official picks from national and state tourism sources (its knowledge base) and popular, highly rated places from a place-data service — and uses an external routing service for travel times within and across states. |
| **Objective O2** | To develop the planner with a conversational preference agent that collects missing trip details, structured fact extraction (hours, closed days, fees, visit duration, location), preference-based retrieval, a language-model itinerary generator, and a constraint checker that rejects plans with closed venues, impossible travel times or over-budget days. |
| **Objective O3** | To evaluate the planner on constraint-violation rate, grounding, fact accuracy, preference-collection accuracy, coverage fairness across states, latency and cost. |
| **Intended Users** | Domestic and international leisure tourists planning a 1–5 day trip in Malaysia (one state, or neighbouring states by road), including families, elderly travellers and users with budget, accessibility or dietary needs. |
| **Supported Tasks** | 1. Collect trip preferences through a short conversation (destination, days, budget, transport, interests, group, pace, accessibility/food needs). <br>2. Generate a day-by-day itinerary with times, stops and travel legs. <br>3. Show each stop's opening hours, fee and source ("Official guide" or "Popular" with its rating) with a "last checked" date. <br>4. Check and report feasibility (opening hours, travel time, budget). <br>5. Revise the plan when the user changes a preference (e.g. "add Ipoh", "make it cheaper"). <br>6. For public-transport legs, show scheduled departure and arrival times from official operator timetables, with a link to the operator's official ticketing site so the user can buy the ticket themselves. <br>7. Plan around the user's existing bookings and fixed plans — booked flights, buses or trains (place and departure/arrival time), hotel location, timed reservations and a few must-visit places (typically 2–3); the system suggests the remaining stops. |
| **Information / Knowledge Domain** | Official Malaysian tourism information: attractions, opening hours, closed days, entry fees, typical visit duration, location and category, from Tourism Malaysia and state tourism bodies. Popular, highly rated places (name, rating, review count, location, live opening hours) from a place-data service (Google Places). Travel time and distance from a routing service. Official public transport timetables (KTMB, Rapid KL / Rapid Penang, BAS.MY) from Malaysia's open-data portal. A verified fee table for ticketed attractions, built in advance by a fee-research agent from official attraction websites and government pages. Full evaluation on 4 states: Penang, Kuala Lumpur, Melaka, Sabah. |
| **Operating Context** | A web application used before a trip (planning at home) and occasionally during the trip on a phone. Single user per session; no account required. |
| **Important Constraints** | **Data:** official sources differ in format and detail by state; hours and fees change over time. **Access:** the routing service has a monthly free usage cap (10,000 requests per SKU per month for route computation); routing results may not be stored long-term under the provider's terms; the place-data service has a similar free cap and its terms allow only the place ID to be stored, so ratings and hours are fetched live and shown with the provider's attribution. **Budget:** student project — near-zero running cost. **Time:** one semester. **Policy:** source documents not redistributed in the public repo; API keys never committed. **Technical:** cross-state routing by road only within Peninsular Malaysia. Official timetables contain schedules only — no fares or seat availability, and there is no public ticketing API, so the system cannot check or buy tickets. Web search is available only through paid or capped search services (Google's own search API is closing to new users), and search results can show outdated or reseller prices, so fees are researched in a batch and verified, not searched live for every user. |
| **Explicit Out-of-Scope Tasks** | Booking or paying for hotels, transport, tickets or tours. Checking ticket prices or seat availability. Searching, comparing or booking flights (users may enter a flight they have already booked, and the plan works around it). Trips outside Malaysia. Changing, cancelling or verifying the user's bookings. Visa, immigration or legal advice. Medical or safety-critical advice (e.g. whether a hike is safe for a heart condition). Recommending places that cannot be verified in the official knowledge base or the place-data service. Guaranteeing that hours or prices are current. |
| **Evaluation Focus** | Does JalanJalanMY produce personalised itineraries that are **grounded** (every stop comes from an official guide or a verified place-data record), **feasible** (no closed venues, achievable travel times, within budget) and **accurate** (correct hours and fees) — consistently across states — at acceptable latency and cost? |
| **Traceability** | O1 → FR3, FR4, NFR2, RAI1 (grounding, sources). O2 → FR1, FR2, FR5, FR6, FR8, FR9, FR10, NFR1, NFR3, RAI7, RAI8, RAI9 (agents, planning, checking, public transport legs, fixed bookings, verified fees). O3 → all NFR and RAI thresholds, evaluated in `week2_evaluation_criteria.md`. |

## Section 1 — Functional Requirements

| # | Requirement — The system SHALL | Scope Boundary — SHALL NOT (where relevant) |
|---|---|---|
| FR1 | Ask the user for any missing trip details (destination state(s), number of days, daily budget, transport mode, interests, travel group, pace, accessibility and dietary needs, and any existing bookings or must-visit places), and confirm a trip summary before generating a plan. | Generate a plan without confirming the trip summary, or ask again for details the user has already given. |
| FR2 | Generate a day-by-day itinerary for 1–5 days showing, for each stop, the planned arrival time, visit duration and travel time from the previous stop. | Plan trips longer than 5 days, trips outside Malaysia, or trips requiring flights. |
| FR3 | Suggest only verified places: attractions in the official-source knowledge base ("Official guide") or popular places from the place-data service that meet a popularity threshold (proposed: rating ≥ 4.2 from ≥ 500 reviews; "Popular on Google Maps, 4.6★"). Show each suggested stop's label, opening hours, entry fee, source reference and "last checked" date. Places the user names that cannot be found in either source may be included, labelled "added by you — details not verified". | Recommend or invent places, events or details not supported by an official source or a place-data record; rank places by rating alone. |
| FR4 | Check every plan before display for: venue open at the planned time, achievable travel time between consecutive stops, total daily cost within the user's budget, and the user's fixed bookings respected with time buffers; and revise or flag any plan that fails a check. | Show a plan containing a known violation without clearly marking it. |
| FR5 | Update the itinerary when the user changes a preference (e.g. adds a state, lowers the budget, changes the pace), and re-run the feasibility checks. | Silently drop previously confirmed preferences when updating. |
| FR6 | Decline requests outside the defined trip-planning scope (bookings, payments, visas, flights, medical or legal questions, non-Malaysian destinations) and redirect the user to an appropriate official channel. | Perform bookings or payments, or give visa, legal or medical advice. |
| FR7 | Present itineraries in clear English suitable for domestic and international tourists, including a "verify before visiting" notice. | — |
| FR8 | For public-transport legs (e.g. KTM, LRT/MRT, buses), show the scheduled departure and arrival times from the operator's official timetable, and a link to the operator's official ticketing page so the user can buy the ticket themselves. | Check seat availability or fares, sell tickets, or book on the user's behalf; collect data from ticketing websites by scraping. |
| FR9 | Accept fixed commitments entered by the user — booked transport (flight, bus or train: station/airport and departure/arrival time), accommodation location, timed reservations and must-visit places — and build the itinerary around them: start each day's activities after arrival plus a buffer, end before departure minus a buffer, and include the user's must-visit places (typically 2–3) or explain why one cannot fit. The remaining time shall be filled with places the system suggests from the knowledge base and the place-data service to match the user's preferences and the location of the fixed points. | Move, change, cancel or verify the user's bookings; silently drop a must-visit place; ask for booking references, ticket numbers or passenger names. |
| FR10 | Show the entry fee for ticketed attractions from a verified fee table built by a fee-research agent. The agent searches the web for each attraction's official price, accepts only official sources (the attraction's own website or government domains), and saves the fee with its source URL, the exact quoted sentence and the date found. Fees not in the table are shown as "check the official site". | Use reseller or blog prices; show a price without a source and date; search the web live for every user request. |

## Section 2 — Non-Functional Requirements

| # | Quality Attribute | Requirement | Measurement Method | Metric | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NFR1 | Performance | The system shall return a complete verified itinerary within an acceptable waiting time, and respond to each preference question quickly. | Run the 40-request evaluation workload (10 per evaluated state, including 10 cross-state trips) and log end-to-end time; separately log time per preference-agent turn. | p90 itinerary latency; p90 agent-turn latency | Itinerary ≤ 20 s; agent turn ≤ 4 s |
| NFR2 | Correctness | Opening hours and entry fees shown in itineraries shall match the official source. | Hand-check a random sample of at least 50 displayed stops against their official source pages. | Fact accuracy (%) | ≥ 95% |
| NFR3 | Reliability | Displayed itineraries shall be feasible: no closed venues, achievable travel times, within budget. | Run the 40-request workload; check each displayed plan with an independent script (opening hours + recomputed travel times + cost total). | % of plans with zero constraint violations | ≥ 95% |
| NFR4 | Cost | Running cost shall fit a student budget. | Record language-model token usage, routing-service calls and place-data calls across the evaluation workload. | Model cost per itinerary; routing and place-data calls per month; search requests per fee-table refresh | ≤ US$0.05 per itinerary (illustrative); routing calls, place-data calls and search requests within the providers' monthly free allowances |
| NFR5 | Maintainability | Updated or newly added official source documents (e.g. a new state) shall be incorporated without code changes to other components. | Controlled update test: replace one state's source document and add one new state's sources; time the process. | Update time; code files changed | ≤ 15 min; 0 code files changed |

## Section 3 — Responsible AI Requirements

| # | Responsible AI Risk | Requirement | Measurement Method | Metric | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RAI1 | Hallucination / Grounding | Every stop the system suggests shall be traceable to an official source in the knowledge base or to a place-data record, with a working source reference and the correct label; user-named places found in neither source shall be clearly labelled as not verified. | For all displayed stops in the evaluation workload, match each suggested stop to a knowledge-base entry or a place-data record and check the source reference and label; check that every user-named place found in neither source carries the label. | Grounded-stop rate; source-reference presence rate | 100% grounded; 100% presence |
| RAI2 | Fairness / Coverage across states | Plan quality shall not differ beyond an agreed tolerance between evaluated states. | Compute NFR3 feasibility rate and preference-match rate separately for Penang, Kuala Lumpur, Melaka and Sabah. | Largest gap between any two states | ≤ 10 percentage points |
| RAI3 | Safety / Scope | The system shall decline out-of-scope or unsafe requests and redirect appropriately, and shall cap daily driving time. | Run a scope/safety test set of at least 20 queries (bookings, visas, medical, flights, foreign destinations, unsafe activities); check all cross-state plans for driving time. | Refusal-and-redirect success rate; plans exceeding the driving cap | ≥ 95% refusal success; 0 plans with > 5 h driving per day |
| RAI4 | Outdated information / Transparency | Every stop shall show a "last checked" date, and stops whose source is older than the freshness limit shall be flagged. | Inspect all displayed stops; compare source dates against the freshness limit. | Date-shown rate; unflagged stale stops | 100% shown; 0 unflagged stops older than 6 months |
| RAI5 | Privacy & Security | The system shall not store personal data from conversations, and API keys shall never appear in the repository or client. | Inspect logs after the test workload for names/phone numbers/emails; run a secret scan on the repository history. | PII items found in logs; secrets found in repo | 0; 0 |
| RAI6 | Misread preferences | The preference agent shall correctly capture the user's stated trip details. | Run 30 scripted conversations with known target values (including vague answers like "not too expensive"); compare the confirmed summary to the targets. | Slot-filling accuracy; average questions per conversation | ≥ 90% accuracy; ≤ 5 questions |
| RAI7 | Timetable changes / Transparency | Every public-transport leg shall match the official timetable, show a "times may change — check with the operator" notice, and link to the official ticketing page. | Check a sample of at least 20 public-transport legs against the operator's published timetable; inspect every leg for the notice and a working official link. | Timetable match rate; legs with notice and working link | ≥ 95%; 100% |
| RAI8 | Missed connections | Plans shall never cause the user to miss a booked flight, bus or train: entered bookings are read back for confirmation, and minimum buffers apply (default: arrive ≥ 90 min before a domestic flight and ≥ 30 min before a train or bus; start activities ≥ 60 min after a flight lands and ≥ 15 min after a train or bus arrives). | Run at least 15 workload requests with entered bookings (including tight and impossible cases); check every plan against the bookings and buffers. | Plans violating a booking or buffer; impossible cases clearly reported to the user | 0; 100% |
| RAI9 | Fee provenance / Hallucination | Every fee in the table shall come from an official source and be backed by a quoted sentence from that source, and the agent's extracted fees shall match the official pages. | Hand-check at least 30 fees from the table against their official pages (ticket type, price, currency); audit every row for an official URL, a quote and a date; check every source domain against the allowlist. | Fee match rate; rows with URL + quote + date; rows from non-official domains | ≥ 95%; 100%; 0 |

## 🤔 Reflection — Which Responsible AI requirement is most difficult to measure or enforce?

**RAI4 (outdated information)** is the hardest to enforce. RAI1 can be enforced in code because a stop either exists in the knowledge base or the place-data service, or it does not. RAI4 depends on facts that change in the real world without telling us: a museum can change its hours or close permanently the day after we collect the data, and the official page itself may be out of date. The system can only show the date we last checked and flag old entries — it cannot know the true current state. Measuring it is also costly, because checking whether the information is still correct needs manual re-verification of each source. This means RAI4 reduces the harm (users are warned) rather than removing it, which is why the "verify before visiting" notice is part of FR7.
